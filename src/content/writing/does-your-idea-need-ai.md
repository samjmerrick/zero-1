Most founders I talk to assume their product should use AI. Some are right. Plenty would build a better business without it, or with far less of it than they planned.

Here’s the test I use. It takes five minutes: five questions, answered without wishful thinking.

## 1. What job does the AI do?

Name the specific task, in one sentence, as if you were writing a job advert. “Drafts a first reply to customer emails” is a job. “Makes the product smarter” isn’t.

If you can’t name the job, you don’t need AI yet. You need to understand the problem better.

## 2. Would a rule do it?

A surprising number of “AI features” are a few if-statements in disguise. If a person could write down the rules in an afternoon, write the rules. They’re cheaper, faster and easier to explain to customers.

## 3. What happens when it’s wrong?

Every model makes mistakes. The question is what a mistake costs. A bad song recommendation costs nothing. A wrong dosage or a missed fraud flag costs a lot. The higher the cost of an error, the more you’ll spend on checks, reviews and evaluation, and the less the AI saves you.

## 4. Does it get better with your data?

This is where the edge comes from. If your product uses the same model, the same way, as everyone else, the model provider can copy you. If it gets better because of data or know-how only you have, that advantage grows as you do.

## 5. Does the maths still work?

AI has a cost every time someone uses it. Work out roughly what one customer’s usage costs you each month, and compare it with what they’ll pay. If the margin only works when nobody uses the feature much, you have a problem.

## Scoring it

If you could name the job, a rule won’t do, mistakes are survivable, your data makes it better and the margins work: build it with AI.

If you stumbled on two or more, start without it. Ship the simplest version, learn what customers value, and add AI where it earns its place.
